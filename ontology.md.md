## Route

A **Route** is the primary organisational object used within **The Route**.

A Route represents a collection of related **Service Patterns** that together form a passenger-recognisable railway service within a single **Network**.

Examples include:

* Oxford Route
* Brighton Route
* North Kent Route
* Cumbrian Coast Route
* South Wales Route

A Route belongs to exactly one Network.

A Route contains one or more Service Patterns.

Service Patterns are assigned to Routes using a combination of Builder-generated analysis and editorial review, ensuring that Routes reflect how passengers and the railway industry naturally understand the network.

Unlike Service Patterns, which are derived objectively from timetable data, Routes provide a stable organisational layer for gameplay, Routepedia and future tooling. Their purpose is to organise operational railway data into coherent passenger-facing groups without changing the underlying operational model.

Routes therefore form the bridge between the Builder's operational railway model and the editorial knowledge maintained by **The Route**.
